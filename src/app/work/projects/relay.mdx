## The problem

About 350 people at a logistics company send requests to four teams, and each request needs the right team, a clear reply and a record of what happened. The tool had to help the team without making decisions for it, and keep one company's data away from another's.

![Relay home page: requests from the office, a site and the road are sorted by Relay and sent to HR, IT, Finance or Operations](/images/projects/relay/home.jpg)

## I started with the rules, not the screens

Before building, I wrote the user stories with Given / When / Then acceptance criteria for each sprint, and each one was tested by hand. Then I wrote who may see, take, assign, close and reopen a request, and what a manager can change, as rules in the database. The screens depend on those rules, not the other way round.

## How it works

_The screenshots show Relay with sample data for Kopano Freight, the fictional company it was built for._

**1. Staff send a request.** They write what they need and choose their depot and how urgent it is. A classifier sends it to a department. Fixed rules mark sensitive requests (harassment, self-harm, injury, a hacked account), and those never go to the AI.

![Staff form for a new request, with subject, details, depot and urgency](/images/projects/relay/submit.jpg)

**2. An agent answers it.** The agent opens the ticket and gets an AI draft in the tone they choose, labelled as AI-drafted. They edit it and send it themselves. The routing panel shows what the AI chose and how sure it was, and the agent can move the ticket if it is wrong.

![Ticket view: the request on the left, an editable AI-drafted reply on the right, and the AI's routing choice with 91% confidence](/images/projects/relay/ticket.jpg)

**3. Managers keep it moving.** They see every ticket by priority and status, with sensitive ones tagged, and approve purchases over R5,000 and all leave. Nobody decides their own request.

**4. Everyone watches the numbers.** The dashboard shows tickets, reply times, open tickets and how many needed a check, with an AI summary written only from those numbers. Weekly reports, a PDF and a workload forecast build on the same figures.

![Manager dashboard: 634 tickets, 6.4 hours to first reply, 33 open, and an AI summary of the period](/images/projects/relay/dashboard.jpg)

**5. Compliance checks the AI.** An audit log records every AI decision and every human change, with flagged replies and a risk report.

## Keeping companies apart

Relay started as one company's help desk and grew to eight companies on one system. The database rules keep each company's people and tickets apart for anyone who is signed in. An audit before the showcase found the gap those rules can't cover: jobs that run with nobody signed in, such as the Monday summaries and reports, automatic assignment and urgent escalation. Row rules don't apply to them, so they counted every company's tickets together and could have notified a manager in another company.

I wrote the fix as one reviewed migration. Every one of those jobs now checks the company itself, and reports and summaries are kept per company. I tested it on the demo database with a second company inside a transaction that was rolled back, so nothing was left behind. I also locked a demo-only reset function on the live database, because it would have cleared every company's open tickets.

I also redesigned the platform admin pages, where Relay's own team adds companies and their first admin. They show each company's people, and never its tickets.

## Built with

**Next.js 16** and **React** with **TypeScript**, styled with **Tailwind CSS** and shadcn/ui. **Supabase** holds the **PostgreSQL** database, sign-in and row-level security on every table. The classifier is a TF-IDF and logistic regression model trained in **Google Colab** with scikit-learn, exported to JSON and run in TypeScript on the server, so no Python server runs in production. Drafts and summaries use the **Google Gemini API**, with the key kept on the server. Email notifications are built on **Resend** and are being switched on, and the site runs on **Vercel**.

## What's next

Automatic assignment and urgent escalation are now live. Next: switch on email notifications, add backup codes for two-step sign-in, and let staff choose a request type on the form so purchases and leave go to a manager on their own.

## What I learned

The hard problems were never the AI. They were about who can see and change what, and the order things happen in. Testing as each role found a manager's list that showed people from every company, and a new person who reached two-step sign-in before choosing a password. The audit found scheduled jobs that counted every company's tickets together. Now I write the rules down first, test as every role, and check anything that runs with nobody signed in.
