## The challenge

The business asked for a case management dashboard, but the requirement was a single vague line. Building straight from it would have produced something that *looked* finished and didn't help anyone. The real question was: **what decisions does each department need this dashboard to support?**

## What I did

1. **Went beyond the original requester.** I consulted other departments to get more perspectives on how cases are raised, handled and reported, and where the current process broke down.
2. **Redesigned the requirement.** I reshaped the dashboard around their feedback instead of building to the first brief, and redesigned features when stakeholder feedback showed the first solution didn't fit.
3. **Wrote it so developers could build it.** User stories with **positive and negative acceptance criteria**, business rules, process maps and system flows, then turned the requirements into development tasks.
4. **Planned the delivery.** I drew up a sprint roadmap so the team could deliver incrementally.
5. **Tested what was built.** Feature testing in staging and UAT, logging bugs, and retesting the fixes.

## What I learned

A vague requirement isn't a spec, it's a prompt to ask more questions. Talking to the people *around* the original requester surfaced needs the first brief would have missed, and that's the habit I've carried into every project since, including my AI prototypes.
